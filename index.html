🇰🇷 Seoul — 24h (May 16–17)
♨ Jjimjilbang — Korean Bathhouse · Must-Do
What it is Bucket List
A jjimjilbang (찜질방) is a 24-hour Korean wellness complex — bathhouse + multiple themed saunas + sleeping room + food court. Entry ₩7,000–15,000. You get cotton pyjamas (jjimjilbok), a locker key, and full access. Naked in the gender-separated bathing pools (fully normal, nobody stares — you'll get used to it in 30 seconds), clothed in the shared sauna/lounge areas. Get the body scrub (세신, seshin) — an attendant scrubs off your entire outer layer of dead skin with a coarse mitt. Sounds brutal, feels incredible. ~₩25,000 add-on. Eat hard-boiled eggs and sikhye (sweet rice drink) after — a Korean spa tradition.
Siloam Bulgama Sauna Best for Tourists · 6 Floors · 3,000 Capacity
Best overall jjimjilbang for first-time foreign visitors. 6 floors covering every kind of sauna — charcoal room, clay room, salt room, Himalayan salt crystal room, ice room, and multiple hot pool temperatures. 24 hours, can sleep overnight (perfect if you want to skip the hostel May 16 and save money). Near Seoul Station — easy AREX connection. Foreigner-friendly, English signage, huge and lively without feeling sterile.
Best time to go: May 16 evening after arriving ICN (it's open 24h — go before or after dinner).
📍 253 Seosomun-ro, Jung-gu · Near Seoul Station · ₩12,000–15,000 · 24h
Insadong Hanjeungmak Most Atmospheric · Near Palace
Traditional Korean pine-heated sauna near Anguk Station — the closest jjimjilbang to Gyeongbokgung Palace. Heated by real yellow archer stones and pine wood in a fireplace, not electric elements. The traditional Korean body scrub here is the standout — deep exfoliation, skin feels silky for days. Smaller, more local crowd, intimate atmosphere.
Best time to go: May 17 morning before or after the palace (opens early).
📍 36 Yulgok-ro 6-gil, Jongno-gu B1 · Near Anguk Station · ₩10,000–13,000
Dragon Hill Spa Most Famous · 8 Floors · Near Itaewon
Seoul's most famous jjimjilbang — 8 floors including movie theatre, arcade, rooftop garden, and crystal salt sauna. Popular with foreign visitors given the Itaewon location and English signage. Good if you want a big, buzzy, resort-like experience. Open 24h. Good pre- or post-Born & Bred dinner option (both in Yongsan area).
📍 40 Hangang-daero 21-gil, Yongsan-gu · Near Yongsan Station · ₩12,000–18,000 · 24h

🗺 Things To Do · Attractions
Gyeongbokgung Palace Start Here · 09:00
The grand Joseon dynasty palace — Korea's most iconic sight. Huge ceremonial courtyards, traditional architecture, and the famous Changing of the Guard ceremony at 10am and 2pm. Get there right at 9am before the crowds arrive. Allow 1.5h.
📍 161 Sajik-ro, Jongno-gu · ₩3,000 · Opens 9am · Closed Tue
Bukchon Hanok Village 15min Walk from Palace
A neighbourhood of 600+ traditional Korean hanok houses on winding hillside alleys — one of the most photographed streetscapes in Asia. Completely free, no entry required. Most beautiful in the early morning light. Walk north from the palace along Bukchon-ro.
📍 Gye-dong, Jongno-gu · Free · 15 min walk from palace
Insadong + Ssamziegil After Bukchon
Seoul's artisan district — pottery, calligraphy, Korean tea houses, street food. Ssamziegil is a spiral courtyard with indie designers, food stalls, and live performances. Great for a quick 45-min browse before heading to lunch.
📍 Insadong-gil, Jongno-gu · Free to walk · 10 min from Bukchon
Gwangjang Market Last Meal Before ICN · 12:00
Korea's oldest continuously operating market (1905). Go straight to the indoor food hall — bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes, fried tableside), mayak gimbap (tiny rice rolls, addictive), yukhoe (beef tartare). One of the best food experiences in Seoul. Cash only, busy, worth it.
📍 88 Changgyeonggung-ro, Jongno-gu · ₩10,000–20,000 · Cash only
Hongdae Night Scene Evening May 16 · After Dinner
Seoul's nightlife and indie culture district — live busking, indie bars, street art, clubs. Walk around Hongdae-ro and follow the music. Luxury Su for cocktails, Wa Bar for casual. Wrap up by midnight — big day tomorrow.
📍 Hongdae, Mapo-gu · Free to walk · Bars ₩10,000–20,000/drink

♨ Onsen + Bathhouses — Singapore
Singapore vs Korea Bathhouse Culture
Singapore doesn't have jjimjilbangs — but has a thriving Japanese-inspired onsen scene, the best in Southeast Asia. Unlike the Korean communal naked bathing culture, Singapore onsens are a mix of communal (gender-separated, nude) and private rooms. Crowd skews young professionals, expats, and couples. Much more upscale and quieter than Korean jjimjilbangs.
Yunomori Onsen & Spa · Kallang Southeast Asia's First · Most Authentic
The closest thing to a Japanese onsen in SG. Gender-separated communal pools (naked, like Japan) — jet bath, silk bath, soda bath, cold plunge, sauna. After soaking, meet in the Yunomori Cafe for ramen and Japanese fare. Very popular with young local professionals and expats. 15 min MRT from CBD (Stadium Station, Circle Line). Easy fit into May 25 afternoon before Candlenut dinner.
📍 Kallang Wave Mall, 1 Stadium Pl #02-17/18 · ~SGD 45–49 day pass · 10am–10pm
G.Spa · Guillemard Road 24 Hours · Best Value
Open 24 hours — ideal if arriving late May 24 and want to decompress after the Bali wedding. SGD $72 covers unlimited access to hot pool, cold pool, steam bath, sauna, relaxation lounge, and unlimited food and drinks at their dining cove (free-flow hot food, fresh fruit, cold-pressed juices). Marble-inspired interiors, opulent for the price. Popular with night-owl locals. Foot bath from SGD $30.
📍 102 Guillemard Road, #02-02 · ~SGD 72 full-day (incl. food) · 24 hours
Ikeda Spa · Bukit Timah Most Prestigious · First Japanese Spa SG
Singapore's first authentic Japanese spa (since 2009), 40+ spa awards. Hinoki cypress wood onsen baths — the same wood used in Japanese imperial temples. Has a Zen garden with tea ceremony. More of a private/couples spa experience than communal — private Hinoki onsen room from SGD $100 (30 min). Best if you want a quiet, premium solo spa session rather than communal pools.
📍 787 Bukit Timah Road · From SGD $100/30min private · 40+ awards

🗺 Things To Do · Must-See
Marienplatz + Old Town First Stop · S-Bahn arrives here
Munich's central square. Neues Rathaus Glockenspiel plays at 11am and noon — a 32-figure mechanical performance, try to time your visit. Frauenkirche twin-tower cathedral steps away. The entire old town is walkable in 1.5h from here.
📍 Marienplatz, Altstadt · S-Bahn from MUC: 40 min, €13.60 day ticket
Asamkirche — Hidden Baroque Masterpiece Hidden Gem · Free · 5min Walk
Tucked away on Sendlinger Strasse, almost no tourists find this. Built by the Asam brothers in 1746 — ornate frescoes, intricate gold stucco work, gilded high altar, all crammed into a tiny space. One of the most beautiful interiors in Germany. Free entry, takes 15 min.
📍 Sendlinger Str. 32 · Free · 5 min walk from Marienplatz
Residenz Munich — Royal Palace Best Indoor Sight
Former royal palace of the Bavarian monarchs — 130 rooms spanning Renaissance, Baroque, Rococo and Neoclassical architecture. One of the most significant art collections in Germany. If you only go inside one building in Munich, this is it.
📍 Residenzstrasse 1, Altstadt · ~€9 · Opens 9am · 5 min from Marienplatz
English Garden + Eisbach River Surfers 20min Walk North
Larger than Central Park. The Eisbach river wave — surfers ride a permanent standing wave in the middle of the city, year-round, daily, for free. Walk 20 min north from Marienplatz or take the U-Bahn (U3/U6). Chinesischer Turm beer garden is inside the park, opens 10am in May.
📍 Englischer Garten, Schwabing · Free to enter
MUCA — Museum of Urban + Contemporary Art Unexpected Gem
Right in Old Town, MUCA houses original works by Banksy, Shepard Fairey, Os Gemeos, and more — one of Europe's most extensive urban art collections. Three floors + the MUCA Bunker next door. A genuine surprise in a city famous for classical museums.
📍 Near Marienplatz · ~€14
Isar River Banks — Local Secret Off Tourist Trail
Walk south from the English Garden along the Isar riverbanks. In May the gravel beaches fill with locals sunbathing, swimming, and drinking beer. Completely off the tourist radar. Combine with a walk over Reichenbachbrücke bridge for the best city-meets-nature views.
📍 Accessible from multiple S-Bahn stops · Free
Olympiapark + Olympic Village If Time Allows · 15min U-Bahn
The former Olympic village bungalows are now sought-after student housing — each tenant given creative control of the exterior, creating a sea of colourful pop-culture murals. The hill has panoramic views of Munich and Alps on clear days. Olympic Tower adds more height.
📍 Olympic Park, Milbertshofen · U3 to Olympiazentrum · Free to walk · Tower ~€13
🍽 Munich Restaurants — Premium + Culturally Rare
Viktualienmarkt Weisswurst Breakfast Most Authentic · 07:30
Weisswurst + sweet mustard + pretzel + Weissbier. Munich is the only city where morning beer is culturally and legally correct. Weisswurst tradition: must be eaten before noon. Market opens 7am, steps from Marienplatz.
📍 Marienplatz · ~€12
Augustiner Bräustuben Skip Hofbräuhaus — Go Here
What Hofbräuhaus pretends to be. Same traditional hall setup, better beer, local crowd, half the noise. This is where Munich residents bring out-of-town friends for real beer hall culture without the tourist performance.
📍 Landsberger Str. 19 · 15 min walk from Marienplatz
Airbräu — MUC Airport Brewery Back at Airport · Airside T2
World's only airport microbrewery, right inside MUC T2 airside. Fresh-brewed Helles, Dunkel, seasonal lagers. Pork knuckle, pretzels. No re-security needed. One of the best airport food experiences in the world.
📍 MUC Terminal 2 Airside · ~€25 · Opens 09:00
